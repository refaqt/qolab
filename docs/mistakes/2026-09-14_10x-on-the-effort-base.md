# 2026-09-14 — The large multiple was built on the effort base, with a ruling as the gate

## What happened

The intended path to a 10× return was `k = 10` on `k × hours × frozen band`, opened over ~10 unpaid years, and “DVB blesses `k = 10`” was treated as the gate before anyone is promised a credit ([token-system item 8](../../token-system-design/analysis/belgian-tax-and-company-law.md), the old “How to file”). The [2026-09-10 review](../log/2026-09-10_arms-length-10x-and-repo-review.md) found both halves wrong: writing the ceiling on hours × rate hands the administration the comparable, so every euro above 1× must be defended as a risk premium on a wage-like benchmark; and the DVB does not confirm a quantum — it confirms the box and hands the number back as a condition.

## Why it went wrong

The repo stated the right test itself (“would an independent have charged about this **for this outcome**?”) and then kept the effort base anyway, because the effort ceiling was already there as the anti-gold-rush brake and the multiple was bolted onto it. The scoped-royalty sketch that carries a 10× without any argument about multiples was filed as a side fork. The filing plan assumed a ruling could settle `k`, which turned an unlikely confirmation into a launch blocker.

## Prevention rule

Do not put a multiple on a base you would have to defend as a risk premium. Put upside on a base whose comparable carries it: a royalty **scoped to the products that embody the contribution** (comparable: royalty rates), or capital at risk (cash shares whose count is not `f(p)`). Keep the effort ceiling for what it is — an arm's-length brake near 1× on time-based work. File DVB for the **box** ((a)–(d)); treat “arm's length as a condition” as success; never make a quantum confirmation a gate. If a large `k` is kept on effort as a fallback, file the ex-ante expected-value argument, evidence it, and write the disallowance allocation before promising anything.
